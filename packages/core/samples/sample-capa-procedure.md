1. Purpose

   This procedure defines the process for identifying, investigating, correcting
   and preventing nonconformities and other quality problems.

2. Scope

   Applies to all products designed, manufactured, serviced or distributed by
   the organization, and to quality problems arising in any process of the
   quality management system.

3. References

   Complaint Handling Procedure. Nonconforming Product Procedure. Risk
   Management Procedure. Change Control Procedure. Management Review Procedure.

4. Definitions

   CAPA — Corrective and Preventive Action. Correction — action to eliminate a
   detected nonconformity. Corrective action — action to eliminate the cause of
   a detected nonconformity. Preventive action — action to eliminate the cause
   of a potential nonconformity.

5. Sources of Quality Data

   5.1 Quality Assurance shall analyze data from complaints, service records,
       returned product, nonconforming product reports, process and in-process
       monitoring, internal and external audit results, supplier performance,
       and post-market surveillance to identify existing and potential causes of
       nonconforming product and other quality problems.

   5.2 The analysis under 5.1 shall be performed at a defined frequency of not
       less than quarterly, and the data sources reviewed shall be recorded.

   5.3 Appropriate statistical methodology shall be employed where necessary to
       detect recurring quality problems, and the method chosen shall be
       identified in the record.

   5.4 Where a trend or signal is identified but no CAPA record is opened, the
       justification for not opening one shall be documented.

6. Initiation and Risk Screening

   6.1 The originator shall record the problem statement, including the quantity
       affected, the date range of occurrence, and the product identifiers
       involved.

   6.2 The originator shall complete an initial risk screening within three
       business days of opening the record, and shall document whether the event
       involves actual or potential patient harm.

   6.3 The level of investigation and action taken shall be proportionate to the
       risk presented by the problem, and the record shall state the basis for
       the level selected.

   6.4 Where the event involves actual or potential patient harm, the record
       shall be escalated for a reportability determination, and the
       determination and its basis shall be recorded in the CAPA record itself.

   6.5 Where nonconforming product is implicated, the record shall reference the
       nonconforming product record and confirm that containment of affected
       product has been addressed.

7. Investigation and Root Cause Analysis

   7.1 The investigation team shall identify the root cause using a structured
       method, and shall name the method used in the record.

   7.2 The record shall document why each candidate cause that was considered
       and eliminated was ruled out, including the evidence relied upon.

   7.3 Where the affected population is bounded by a product configuration,
       software version, lot range or date range, the investigation shall
       explain why that boundary is the correct one.

   7.4 Where the investigation concludes that the cause is human error, the
       record shall document why the underlying process, training or design did
       not prevent the error.

   7.5 The investigation shall determine whether the same cause could affect
       other products, processes or lots, and shall record the extent-of-
       condition assessment and its outcome.

8. Action Planning

   8.1 Each action shall be classified as a correction, a corrective action or a
       preventive action, and each shall name a responsible owner and a target
       completion date.

   8.2 The actions selected shall address the root cause identified in Section 7,
       and the record shall explain how each action addresses that cause.

   8.3 Where no corrective action is taken, the justification shall be
       documented and approved at the same level required for closure.

9. Implementation and Change Control

   9.1 Where an action changes a product, process, specification, or procedure,
       the record shall reference the change control record and shall confirm
       the change was assessed against the risk management file.

   9.2 Changes to methods and procedures needed to correct and prevent the
       identified problem shall be implemented and recorded, including the
       revision of each affected document.

   9.3 Where a design or hardware change is applied to new production only, the
       record shall document the disposition of units already distributed.

   9.4 Personnel affected by a changed method or procedure shall be trained
       before the change takes effect, and the training shall be recorded.

10. Effectiveness Verification

    10.1 The effectiveness acceptance criterion shall be defined and approved
         before the corrective action is implemented.

    10.2 Effectiveness shall be verified against objective evidence of a change
         in the observed failure rate, measured over a minimum observation
         window of ninety days following implementation. Confirmation that an
         action was completed shall not by itself constitute effectiveness
         verification.

    10.3 The verification sample size shall be justified with respect to the
         failure rate the action is intended to reduce.

    10.4 The record shall confirm that the action taken did not adversely affect
         the finished device, and shall identify the evidence relied upon for
         that conclusion.

    10.5 Where effectiveness is not demonstrated, the record shall not be closed
         and the investigation shall be reopened or escalated.

11. Communication and Escalation

    11.1 Information relating to quality problems and nonconforming product
         shall be disseminated to those directly responsible for assuring the
         quality of such product or for preventing such problems, and the
         distribution shall be recorded.

    11.2 Open CAPA records, their ageing, and their effectiveness status shall
         be submitted for management review at each scheduled management review
         meeting.

    11.3 Where an action affects a supplier, the supplier shall be notified and
         the notification and the supplier's response shall be recorded.

12. Preventive Action

    12.1 Potential nonconformities shall be identified from the data sources in
         Section 5 before a nonconformity occurs, and the need for preventive
         action shall be evaluated and recorded.

    12.2 A preventive action record shall document the potential problem, its
         potential effect, and the rationale for the action taken or not taken.

13. Closure

    13.1 The record shall not be closed until every action item has a documented
         outcome and the effectiveness verification is complete and approved.

    13.2 Where ongoing monitoring is required after closure, the record shall
         identify the successor record or monitoring activity by document
         number.

    13.3 Closure shall be approved by the Quality Manager, and the approval date
         shall not precede the date of the evidence relied upon.

    13.4 A CAPA record open longer than one hundred eighty days shall have a
         documented status update and a revised target date approved by the
         Quality Manager.

14. Records

    All records generated under this procedure, and the results of all
    activities performed under it, shall be documented and retained for the
    retention period defined in the records control procedure, and shall be
    filed under the record type "CAPA Record" rather than as meeting minutes or
    general correspondence.
